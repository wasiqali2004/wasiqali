07 — Build

Life Dashboard.

A dashboard I built for myself, and still build on most weeks. Prayer, memorisation, habits, goals, training, spending and the Sabr storefront all report into one screen, with a Claude layer that can read across them. It runs locally, against my own database — it isn't a hosted product and isn't for sale.

Next.js 16TypeScriptDrizzle ORM libSQL / SQLiteTailwind v4Built with Claude

One screen, no scrolling

  1. Problem

    Prayer, workouts, spending and the store each lived in a different app, so none of it got looked at together.

  2. Built

    Eight widgets over one local database, each with its own refresh. Prayer times are computed offline.

  3. Result

    The whole grid fits one viewport on desktop, so nothing is more than a glance away.

Time back

  1. Problem

    Prayer, training, money and the store each meant opening another app and scrolling through it.

  2. Built

    One screen that pulls it together, including a Hevy sync of 171 workouts with a rotatable body map and a Push, Pull and Legs calendar.

  3. Result

    3 hours less scrolling every day.

The build

  1. Problem

    Every data source had its own login, format and sync rules, and none of them talked to each other.

  2. Built

    Integrations with Hevy, Plaid, Yahoo Finance, Shopify, Google Health and Quran.com, over one typed database with 12 migrations. The Quran reader checks every surah’s verse count and rejects any mismatch.

  3. Result

    14,000+ lines of TypeScript across 86 files, built in under three weeks.

User journey

From first question to a working dashboard.

Ten screens of onboarding in Istiqamah, the product version of this dashboard. The answers decide which widgets a new user starts with, then they arrange the page themselves.

09 — Off the clock

On patience.

Sabr means patience. Not waiting — patience. The kind you need when the outcome isn't in your hands and the work still has to be done properly.

I named the brand that before I understood how much it would ask of me. Most of what I've learned since has come from the slow parts: a supplier who took four months to get a finish right, a market where I sold two pieces, a mailing list built a hundred people at a time. I'd rather make something that holds up than something that moves quickly. That isn't a strategy. It's just what I think good work is.
